## Gaps the coach should raise
- **The Stafford scores below 50% (11–13).** Check which White replies cause the losses: 4.Nxc6 dxc6 5.d3 / 5.Nc3, and 3...Nc6 lines where White declines.
- **After 2...Nf6, the lines that are neither Stafford nor Traxler:** 3.d4 (Petrov Steinitz line), 3.Nc3 (Four Knights, or 3...Nc6 transpositions), and 3.Bc4 Nc6 4.d3 (the most common club reply to the Two Knights, where the Traxler never happens).
- **The Englund after White plays correctly:** 2.dxe5 Nc6 3.Nf3 Qe7 4.Qd5 / 4.Nc3 / 4.Bf4 Qb4+ 5.Bd2! And 2.d5 or 2.e4 (declining), 2.c4 exd4, and 2.Nf3.
- **As White vs non-1...e5:** the Scandinavian (most frequent), the Caro-Kann (scoring poorly), the Sicilian (c3 system; fine), and the French.
- **Blitz is where the games are (80%).** Coaching must be blitz-aware: time-trouble mistakes, simple reliable plans, and pattern recognition over deep theory.
- **Gambits and trap openings** score well against unprepared opponents but get punished by prepared ones. Track the score per line **by opponent rating band** and say honestly when a line stops working.

## Key ideas to cover per opening (to be expanded with verified lines)
- **Ponziani:** 3...Nf6 4.d4 (the main fight), 3...d5 4.Qa4, and 3...f5 (Jaenisch). Plans: build the d4 center; the Qa4 pin ideas.
- **Scandinavian:** choose 2...Qxd5 (then 3.Nc3 Qa5/Qd6/Qd8) or 2...Nf6. Plans: quick ...Bf5/...Bg4, ...c6, ...e6 setup.
- **Stafford:** 4.Nxc6 dxc6. The traps after 5.d3 Bc5 and 5.e5, the Oh-No-My-Queen trap, and the sound defensive setups White uses to refute it.
- **Traxler:** 5.Nxf7 Bxf2+ and 5.Bxf7+ Ke7. Heavy calculation; know the forcing lines cold.
- **Englund:** 2.dxe5 Nc6 3.Nf3 Qe7 4.Bf4? Qb4+ (the famous trap). What to do after White's correct 4.Qd5 or 4.Nc3.
